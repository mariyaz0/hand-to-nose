# Decision log (v2)

Short record of what was decided and why. Newest decisions are at the bottom.
Decisions about the archived v0.1 Android app are not repeated here.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | 9 Nov | **Goal:** from one movement (to the nose and back), tell which hand made it. | Meeting with Prof. Bar-Gad. |
| D-02 | 9 Nov | A high sampling rate is not needed; ~60 samples per second is enough. | The movement is slow. |
| D-03 | 10 Nov | **Web app** instead of the Android app, for both data collection and the final test. The Android app v0.1 goes to `archive/`. | One code for everything; works on Android and iPhone; participants join by link; ~60 Hz in the browser is enough (D-02). |
| D-04 | 10 Nov | Low beep **at the moment the nose touches** the screen. | Simple and clear for the participant. |
| D-05 | 10 Nov | Next trial starts after the phone is still + a **fixed 2 s pause** (no random wait). The only button is "Start" at the beginning. | Reaction time is not needed for the goal. Movements that start before the beep are caught and repeated (D-09). |
| D-06 | 10 Nov | **One grip for everyone:** phone upright, lying in the palm, screen to the face. | The sensor axes must mean the same thing for all people. |
| D-07 | 10 Nov | Nose-to-dot alignment only by instruction and the dot (no camera). | Simple; no camera permission. |
| D-08 | 10 Nov | A touch counts only **inside a zone around the dot**; other touches are logged and ignored. | Fingers holding the phone can touch the edge of the screen. |
| D-09 | 10 Nov | Moved before the beep, or no touch within **10 s** → attempt discarded, message, repeat. Movement duration is not filtered. | Keep only valid trials; the classifier must work at any speed. |
| D-10 | 10 Nov | **6 trials per hand, 2 per speed** (slow / normal / fast), random order. Two blocks: one hand, rest, the other hand; first hand random. | Speeds vary on purpose, so the classifier does not depend on speed. Short session, the extended arm gets tired. |
| D-11 | 10 Nov | Participant data: consent, age, sex, short Edinburgh inventory; device info automatic; anonymous random code. | Describe the sample and handedness without names. |
| D-12 | 10 Nov | Explanation: animated diagram + 1–2 practice trials (not saved). | People do the task alone, by link. |
| D-13 | 10 Nov | Interface in English with a switch to Russian (top-right). | Participants. |
| D-14 | 10 Nov | **Final test:** Prof. Bar-Gad does one movement there and back on the S24 Ultra; the screen shows the hand + confidence; no calibration. If time allows, also make it work on his own phone. | This is the final exam of the trial task. |
| D-15 | 10 Nov | Analysis: explicit features + a simple model (logistic regression / decision tree), tested leave-one-subject-out. A small neural network only for comparison. | 10–30 participants expected; the result should be explainable. |
| D-16 | 11 Nov | **Storage: Supabase.** The browser may only insert data; it cannot read, change or delete it. A trial can be added only to an existing session younger than 3 hours. | Data from many phones in one place, safe even though the link is public. |
| D-17 | 11 Nov | Participant data never go into git. | Privacy. |
| D-18 | 11 Nov | A trial starts only when the phone is **still and upright** for 1 s. | A phone lying on a table must not start a trial (e.g. during the rest). |
| D-19 | 11 Nov | Detection thresholds (stillness, movement before the beep) are first guesses, kept in `web/js/config.js`; check them on the first real recordings with `?debug=1`. | No real web recordings yet. |
| D-20 | 11 Nov | Practice: 2 trials at normal speed with the hand of block 1. Rest between blocks: 20 s countdown, then automatic start. | Only one button ("Start practice") during the task. |
| D-21 | 11 Nov | Web app in plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript (no framework, no build step), published on GitHub Pages from the `gh-pages` branch. | Simple to read and to change; nothing to install. |

| D-27 | 11 Nov | **Participants so far:** P1 = `F50F9F36`, P2 = `A56549CD` (iPhone, app 2.1.0, 12 trials each). Pilot `B34CF91D` is excluded. | First two people who did the final protocol. |

| D-41 | 14 Nov | **Sessions used:** only complete study sessions (12 of 12 trials, `is_pilot = false`). Partial sessions (`FB098F48`, `715DDCEB`, `31A22EAB`: 4 trials, one hand only) are not used. | A simple rule, fixed in advance. The partial sessions add little and have only one hand. |
| D-42 | 14 Nov | **No person is excluded for how they did the task.** Participants with many discarded attempts or touches outside the zone, participants who did not follow the speeds, the mixed-handed participant and sessions with a high audio latency all stay. | This is real variation between people. | D-43 | 14 Nov | **Trials are not removed by duration or speed.** A trial is removed only if the movement cannot be found in the sensor data (no clear start, nose touch or return); every removed trial is listed with its reason. | D-44 | 14 Nov | **Not used as features:** the speed label and the reaction time (high beep → start of the movement). | D-45 | 14 Nov | **Missing gyroscope** (`C325FA5F`, `DFDDE9AA`: no rotation rate, the phones have no gyroscope): missing values are **not filled in**. The main model uses features from the raw acceleration with gravity (`ax`, `ay`, `az`), which exists for every phone; the phone tilt comes from the direction of gravity, computed with the same low-pass filter for all phones (not the browser's own split into gravity and movement). A second model adds rotation-rate features and is trained on the participants with a gyroscope. Both are compared leave-one-subject-out; the final test uses the second model only if it is clearly better (the S24 Ultra has a gyroscope). | Zeros or averages in place of rotation would look like a special kind of movement to the model. Without a gyroscope the browser probably estimates gravity more roughly, so its split is not comparable between phones. First check (14 Nov): the net tilt change in the screen plane from start to nose touch is larger for the left hand than for the right in 17 of 25 people, but only by a few degrees and with large overlap, while the mean rotation rate `gx` during the reach was negative for the left hand in all 6 people checked on 13 Nov (and positive for the right hand in 4 of the 5 with right-hand trials). So the acceleration-only model is probably weaker; the comparison shows by how much. |
| D-46 | 14 Nov | Before computing features, each trial is **resampled to an even time grid and low-pass filtered**. | On some Android phones 6–28% of samples repeat the previous reading, so their real rate is below 60 Hz; there are 17 uneven intervals in about 180,000 samples (none during the reach). An arm movement has almost no content above ~10 Hz. |

| D-51 | 14 Nov | **Rotation-rate axes and sign.** The stored columns hold: `gz` = rotation about x, `gx` = rotation about y, `gy` = rotation about z (the browser's `rotationRate.alpha`, `beta`, `gamma` are about x, y, z, not as the comment in `web/js/sensors.js` said). The data are not changed; the analysis renames them `rot_x`, `rot_y`, `rot_z`. The sign is the same on iPhone and Android, so the rotation rate is **not flipped** for iPhone (closes the open point of D-36). | Check on 333 reaches (14 Nov, Supabase SQL): the rotation integrated from the high beep to the nose touch against the change of the gravity direction (fused gravity = with gravity − without gravity). `gz` vs the tilt in the y-z plane: slope −0.99 to −1.07, correlation −0.93 to −0.99 on Chrome, Samsung Internet and Safari; `gy` vs the tilt in the x-y plane: slope 0.69–0.95, correlation 0.71–0.92. `gx` shows no relation to gravity, as expected for a rotation about y: with the phone upright, y points along gravity. So the "`gx`" of the 13 Nov first look is the turn of the phone about its long (vertical) axis, which the accelerometer cannot see. |
| D-52 | 14 Nov | A reach counts as a movement only if the phone **turned at least 4° in total** during it (sum of the absolute rotation rate over the reach); otherwise the trial is dropped as "phone did not move" (D-43). Replaces the first guess of the code (peak rotation ≥ 5 deg/s). | Found while looking at the worst-predicted people of the first model run (23 people, 266 reaches, 94.0 % correct leave-one-subject-out); the threshold was then set from the distribution of the total turn, not from the accuracy. In 19 reaches the phone barely moved: all 12 of `75C535DA` and 7 of `E3EB0DDA` (all 6 right-hand and 1 left-hand reach). They turned 0.2–2.4° in total, the size of the acceleration changed by at most 0.45 m/s², most lasted 0.3–1.0 s: the dot was touched without bringing the phone to the nose (probably the head moved, or the phone was held close). The next reach turned 5.1°, all others at least 14.5°. So the "slightly negative rotation of the left-hander's right hand" in the 13 Nov first look came from these non-movements. |
| D-53 | 14 Nov | **Final model for the test mode: C** = logistic regression on one feature, the net turn of the phone about its long axis (`rot_y_angle`) during the reach. Model version `hand-v1`, trained on all 22 people / 257 reaches; written to `web/js/hand_model.js` by `notebooks/02_hand_model.ipynb`. When reporting, both numbers are given: the pre-planned 9-feature model A 94.2 % (242/257) and C 95.7 % (246/257), chosen after that comparison. | Masha's decision. The three versions differ by 4–5 reaches out of 257, within noise; almost all of A's weight is on this feature. C is the simplest to explain (a left-hand movement is the mirror image of a right-hand one, so the phone turns the other way), does not depend on the speed, is hardly affected by smoothing or repeated readings, and is the easiest to compute in the browser. Its confidence is easy to read (a turn of 5° gives about 90 %). The feature was already seen on 13 Nov, before any model. Pilot sessions: 52/52, including Prof. Bar-Gad's 4 reaches on the S24 Ultra. |
| D-54 | 14 Nov | **Test mode** (app 2.3.0), opened with `?mode=test`: sensors and sound check, then one trial with the same protocol as data collection (still → pause → high beep → nose touch → back → still), then the answer screen (hand, confidence shown at most 99 %, net turn about the long axis) and "Again". The answer is computed in the browser by `web/js/classify.js`, which repeats `analysis/features.py` step by step; a test checks that it gives the same numbers (on all 357 recorded trials the largest difference was 1e-13°). Each test movement is saved as a `test` session (no consent screen, no questionnaire) and a trial with `hand`/`speed` NULL and `predicted_hand`, `predicted_prob` (probability of the answer shown), `model_version`. Data collection is unchanged. | The final exam (D-14). Reusing the collection trial keeps the movement the same as in the training data. Hidden behind a URL parameter so participants never open it by mistake. Saving the test runs keeps a record of the exam. A new test session is started after 38 trials or 2.5 h, because the database accepts trials only for sessions younger than 3 h with fewer than 40 trials. |
